# SetQ product marketing context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-10-08
**Basis:** User instructions, source inspection and primary-source research. Audience and buying objections are informed hypotheses, not customer interview findings.

## Product overview

**One-liner:** SetQ is the AI native operating system for gyms, built around intelligence from the gym floor.

**What it does:** SetQ connects proposed retrofit equipment sensors with a workspace for understanding equipment activity, comparing locations and planning what comes next. The intended Claude experience turns that evidence into plain-language answers and practical followup. The marketing website is an early-access product preview; the current source projects are prototypes, not a qualified deployed product, and Claude integration is in development.

**Category:** Gym operations intelligence. The broader operating-system positioning is user-requested and should remain anchored in equipment, floor planning and operational decisions.

**Product type:** Connected hardware plus a cloud software platform, in development.

**Business model:** Not finalised. No published price, free trial, installation guarantee or subscription terms have been approved.

**Site:** https://setq.com.au
**Demo enquiries:** founder@setq.com.au

## Target audience

**Companies:** Independent commercial gyms, strength-focused clubs and gym groups with several sites; Australia is the initial geographic context, not an exclusive market claim.

**Decision-makers:** Gym owners, general managers, operations leaders and equipment/capital investment managers.

**Primary use case:** Understand how the existing floor is used before changing equipment, layout or investment priorities.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Find where equipment activity concentrates over the week.
- Compare matching assets and periods across locations.
- Decide what to investigate, move, add or retire with evidence and financial assumptions visible.
- Turn the weekly review into a brief and a shortlist of actions.

## Problems and pain points

**Core problem:** Entrance counts and membership reports do not reveal the complete story of individual equipment activity.

**Alternatives:** Staff observations and informal feedback capture important context but are hard to compare across hours and sites. Gym management systems primarily describe membership, payments and schedules. Connected equipment ecosystems can provide valuable equipment data but have their own supported product scope. These are category observations, not claims that every competitor lacks a feature.

**Potential costs:** Time spent assembling reviews, capital decisions made with limited evidence, and floor space allocated without a consistent view of activity. SetQ has no measured savings or causal retention result yet.

**Emotional tension:** Owners want confidence that the next investment serves their members and their business.

## Competitive landscape

**Direct category reference:** GYMetrix / TrackMyGym uses equipment sensing and surveys to inform equipment decisions. Its historical published case studies establish an existing category; do not claim SetQ invented equipment analytics or outperforms it.

**Adjacent:** EGYM Business Suite provides location-level analytics for its ecosystem. SetQ's planned retrofit approach is a different deployment route, not a validated superiority claim.

**Secondary:** Gymflow and other gym management systems handle administration, billing and booking. SetQ should show floor intelligence without claiming an implemented CRM or billing replacement.

**Indirect:** Spreadsheets, manual floor walks and member conversations. These remain useful contextual inputs; sensor movement alone cannot replace customer feedback.

## Differentiation

**Intended differentiators:**

- Start with the equipment a gym already has, subject to installation suitability.
- Bring floor data and a conversational review into the same workspace.
- Keep scope and missing data visible alongside comparisons.
- Connect activity to investment planning without turning motion into invented revenue.

**Why this matters:** A gym operator can move from a scattered impression of the floor to a repeatable review and a clearer shortlist of decisions.

**Proof status:** These are positioning and design intentions. No customer choice, comparative performance, patented technology, production reliability or market-leading claim has been established.

## Objections and fit

| Likely objection | Honest response |
| --- | --- |
| Does it work with our machines? | The retrofit approach starts with compatible weight-stack equipment. A site and fit assessment is required; no universal compatibility promise. |
| Will it watch or identify members? | The proposed equipment sensor observes stack movement. It uses no camera or microphone and does not identify members. Full platform data policies must be defined before deployment. |
| Does a chart prove a machine should be replaced? | Activity is one input. Member feedback, equipment condition, floor constraints and financial assumptions still matter. |
| Is the assistant already live? | The website demonstrates the intended workflow with sample answers. Claude integration is in development. |

**Poor initial fit:** Buyers expecting a mature payments, access-control or CRM replacement; tracking member identity; automatic weight lifted; exact queues; universal equipment support; or a validated autonomous purchasing system.

## Switching dynamics

**Push:** Equipment reviews feel fragmented; comparable data is hard to obtain.
**Pull:** A floor view, a plain-language brief and an equipment review in one place.
**Habit:** Existing staff routines, spreadsheets and long-standing supplier relationships.
**Anxiety:** Installation effort, equipment fit, ongoing cost, data trust and whether the insights change a decision.

## Customer language

There are no verified SetQ interview quotes. Suggested questions below are author-written prompts, never testimonials:

- Which machines see the most activity during the evening peak?
- What should I review before the next equipment order?
- What changed on the floor this week?
- Are comparable machines used differently across our locations?

**Use:** Your floor, equipment activity, weekly brief, peak periods, locations, compare, review, plan, observed period, coverage.
**Avoid:** Revolutionary, seamless, supercharge, autonomous gym, guaranteed ROI, instant retrofit, real-time occupancy, exact queue, identify members, every machine, never miss a set.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Equipment activity | A descriptive umbrella; the underlying metric must remain defined |
| Away from rest time | Existing source1 metric: time a qualified moving part is away from rest; stationary holds count |
| Coverage | The period for which the required observation evidence is available; unknown is not zero |
| Activity share | For a sample display, away-from-rest time divided by observed equipment time; not member occupancy |
| Product preview | Illustrative website interaction, not a live connected gym or model call |
